- Annotate readings inline in every translation, lines and vocab alike:
  write each kanji run as 漢字[かんじ] — the run followed immediately by its
  hiragana reading in square brackets. Kana and katakana get no annotation.
  Okurigana stay outside the brackets: 抜[ぬ]く, never 抜く[ぬく].
  Example: 出汁[だし]は大丈夫[だいじょうぶ]ですか？
- Also return "lineRomanizations" and "vocabRomanizations": arrays of strings
  corresponding exactly by index and count to "lines" and "vocab". Romanize
  the Japanese you actually wrote, not the English source or a paraphrase.
- Use modified Hepburn spelling: shi, chi, tsu, fu, ji, sha/shu/sho,
  cha/chu/cho, ja/ju/jo. Preserve loanword sounds such as vi in ヴィ.
- Separate words and grammatical particles with spaces. Keep inflected word
  endings attached (tabemasu, tabenai); separate desu, particles, and auxiliary
  iru (tabete imasu). Do not split syllables or insert spaces into Japanese text.
- Read particles by their grammatical function: topic は = wa, directional
  へ = e, object を = o. Elsewhere retain ha/he (haha, hana, heya).
- Use macrons ā ī ū ē ō for long vowels, including katakana ー:
  ビーガン = bīgan, コーヒー = kōhī, スーパー = sūpā.
  Use ō for a long おう/おお sound and ū for long うう (Tōkyō, kūkō).
  Retain conventional ei and ii spellings (sensei, oishii, iie); do not collapse
  vowels across word/morpheme boundaries (omou, not omō).
- Small っ doubles the following consonant (kitte); before ch use tch
  (matcha). Write syllabic ん as n, using an apostrophe before a vowel or y
  to avoid ambiguity (kin'en, shin'yō). Do not change it to m before b/m/p.
- Capitalize sentence starts and proper names; vocabulary stays lowercase
  unless a proper name. Use Latin punctuation, not Japanese punctuation.
  Examples:
  私[わたし]はビーガンです。 → Watashi wa bīgan desu.
  母[はは]はコーヒーを飲[の]みます。 → Haha wa kōhī o nomimasu.
  東京[とうきょう]へ行[い]きます。 → Tōkyō e ikimasu.
