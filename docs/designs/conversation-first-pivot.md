# Design: Conversation-first phrasebook — multi-conversation generation + three-tab review

## Problem Statement

`/textbook` was changed to generate content conversation-first: it designs a canonical two-sided
dialogue, then distills phrases and words from it. Output quality jumped markedly. But the API
presents what is generatively primary as presentationally terminal — the conversation is emitted as
the last of up to eight groups, and the UI's unit of attention stays the individual card group. The
durable unit (cards) and the valuable unit (the conversation) disagree, which is the root of the
mental block around the P2 (card/group expansion) UI: every expansion affordance pulls toward the
card and away from the conversation that made the content good.

The pivot: bring the conversation to the fore in the UX and data model, without reintroducing
bookkeeping, a fully editable canvas, or an unbounded branching structure.

## What Makes This Cool

"Branching" — the thing that made a single canonical conversation feel wrong for prep — is not a
representation problem. A conversation only branches while it hasn't happened yet; the moment you
review it, it's linear. So the design flattens anticipated branches into **several short, parallel,
linear conversations**, each a distinct scene along the encounter's timeline (before / during /
after). You keep the anti-Duolingo learning mechanic (decompose a whole conversation into parts, not
construct from blocks), every conversation stays readable and mobile-friendly, and the phrasebook
reads like the arc of a real encounter instead of a pile of cards.

## Constraints

- No bookkeeping: the user never files, reorders, or regroups. The app files via `context`.
- Opinionated and bounded: no reroll, no fully editable canvas, no slot-machine lever.
- Thin schema: prove new richness in `notes` before promoting it to a field.
- Pane Protocol: exactly two stacking layers, no primitive for unbounded push depth — so no
  navigable conversation tree.
- Backend-independent prompt; existing caps, validation, reading normalization, CORS, and usage
  accounting are reused.

## Premises (confirmed this session)

- **Time, not structure, decides lens-vs-artifact.** A conversation that already happened (an
  ingested transcript) is a linear artifact; a conversation that hasn't happened (prep) is a lens
  onto portable language. Current scope is prep → lens: cards stay durable; the conversation is
  represented as the returned groups, and no separate canonical conversation is retained.
- **Branching is anticipation, realized as parallel linear scenes** — not a tree. Each anticipated
  fork is its own short conversation; no new pane-protocol layer.
- **The encounter timeline is the phrasebook spine.** Conversations map loosely to before/during/
  after; the single-conversation causal arc is promoted to an across-conversation ordering.
- **Checklist = the set of proposed conversations (1:1).** Reverses the prior "checklist is not a
  required group title" contract. Each checked item generates exactly one conversation; Call 1 is
  responsible for combining overlapping paths (e.g. accept vs. politely decline) into a single
  candidate title, and Call 2 never merges selected titles. Titles are goal-revealing where possible
  ("Compliments and offering help") over purely temporal ("Before the meal"); both are valid.
- **Decomposition is the single engine.** The deferred transcript-ingest job and the prep job feed
  the same "decompose one linear conversation into reviewable parts" primitive; they differ only in
  source and whether the spine is retained.

## Recommended Approach — "Timeline of scenes"

**Status of this record.** The *product model* below — timeline-of-scenes generation, the three-tab
shell, global-pooled vocab, and Review-on-Starred — is the committed design direction. The exact
*wire contract* (field names, discriminators, caps) is to be finalized against real `/textbook`
output; where a shape is named here it is the intended target, not yet a settled contract. A
*conversation* is a group whose cards are ordered turns; it has no separate stored object.

**Generation**

- **Call 1 (clarify).** Select-questions hone context (unchanged). The checklist becomes an ordered
  set of candidate conversation titles spanning the encounter's before/during/after timeline; titles
  are goal-revealing where possible. The model reasons in temporal order internally. Unchecked items
  remain a suppression signal for later expansion (carried from the prior design).
- **Call 2 (generate).** For each checked title, generate one conversation, emitted as one group in
  the checked-selection order. Vocab is returned as a **separate top-level response field**, not a
  group, so it neither consumes the existing eight-group cap nor risks end-of-response truncation.
  The client stores the result; the service stays stateless.

**Conversation shape**

- 4–8 turns, target 6; under 4 too thin, over 8 too long.
- Two-sided (both speakers, likely replies), biased to single sentences; break only where two
  utterances are naturally one turn ("Hello! How are you?").
- Value-dense and distinct: every turn carries a distinct valuable phrase. Bias toward one longer
  coherent conversation over several short redundant ones — a valuable phrase belongs as a turn in a
  longer distinct conversation rather than a unique turn padded by redundant turns in a short one.
- **Fold complementary yes/no paths into one conversation** when they overlap substantially — done at
  Call 1 candidate generation, so a folded pair arrives as a single checked title. Salsa: ask to
  dance → accept → compliment → thank → ask again → politely decline — one conversation carrying both
  the affirmative and the refusal, not two near-duplicates.

**Ordering**

- **Call 1 array order is authoritative.** Checklist order encodes the before/during/after timeline;
  selection filters but never reorders it, and Call 2 emits conversation groups in that same filtered
  order. The client preserves it. Initial order helps the user grasp the arc on first review.
- Conversations added *after* creation (via later expansion — out of P1 scope) append out of timeline
  order. Acceptable, because by then the user knows the set; the exact add-conversation contract is
  deferred to the expansion design.

**Vocabulary**

- A single global pooled set (top-level response field), surfaced as one Vocab tab — not
  per-conversation.
- Includes nouns, adjectives, and verbs; verbs in infinitive form.
- Each vocab entry carries a `notes` back-reference to the source conversation/phrase it came from
  (multi-source shape is an open question below).

**Review shell — three tabs**

- **Conversations** — the scenes in timeline order; the primary read/immerse surface and the default
  tab. Named "Conversations," not "Phrases."
- **Vocab** — the global pooled decomposition output.
- **Starred** — both starred phrases and starred vocab. Every conversation turn and every vocab entry
  is a star-able card with one shared identity across tabs (starring in Conversations surfaces the
  same card here). The **Review** action operates only on Starred cards, launches from the Starred
  tab, and is disabled when nothing is starred. Unstarred cards stay browsable in Conversations and
  Vocab; Review is the only surface scoped to Starred.

**Storage**

- Client-side only; the API remains stateless and holds nothing. The client stores, as phrasebook
  metadata, the Call 1 context (select-question answers and the checked titles) alongside the
  returned conversation groups and pooled vocab. There is no separate canonical conversation object.

Cost/shape: reuses the `/textbook` two-call flow, `context` auto-filing, star, and group rendering.
Net-new: call-1 checklist reframed as conversation titles; call-2 multi-conversation generation +
pooled vocab extraction with source back-refs; a three-tab content shell; Review scoped to Starred.

### Contract deltas (to apply when accepted)

Backward-incompatible changes this pivot makes to the current authoritative docs:

- `API_DESIGN.md` `/textbook` call 1: checklist items become **conversation titles**, not
  communicative micro-goals; the list is ordered (timeline) and 1:1 with generated conversations.
- `API_DESIGN.md` call 2: **multiple scene conversations** replace the single canonical conversation
  + derived groups + final example-conversation group; vocab moves to a **top-level field**, not a
  group; conversation groups emit in checked-selection order.
- `BRIEF.md` current experience: the **Review** action is **scoped to Starred** (whole-phrasebook
  browsing stays in the Conversations/Vocab tabs); phrasebook content is a set of conversations + a
  pooled vocab list rather than sections + one example dialogue.

These are recorded here; the current docs are updated only when this direction is accepted.

### Approaches ruled out during the session

- **Conversation as durable artifact (for prep).** Real prep conversations diverge, so a single
  retained script is the wrong durable object. Retained as the right model for the *deferred*
  transcript-ingest job only.
- **Navigable branching tree.** Violates the Pane Protocol's no-unbounded-depth rule and
  reintroduces bookkeeping. Branches become parallel linear scenes instead.
- **Single canonical conversation (status quo).** Buries divergence and the arc in one long dialogue;
  the timeline-of-scenes distributes the arc and surfaces divergence as distinct scenes.
- **Checkbox = micro-goal, model clusters into scenes.** Rejected in favor of checkbox = scene (1:1);
  the user shapes at scene grain, the model fills each scene's goals.

## Open Questions

- **P2 depth (deferred to Figma).** Diving into a conversation's parts — grammar highlighting,
  showing other phrases with the same pattern, per-card easier/harder and explanation depth — is the
  original P2 and remains unshaped; the user will explore it in Figma before finalizing here. It must
  stay within the two-layer Pane Protocol (recenter-in-place, not push-depth) and the
  no-reroll/bounded constraints.
- **Generation budget (per backend).** Several two-sided conversations plus pooled vocab in one serial
  call 2 will fight the tightest provider ceilings — Claude Haiku 8,192 and GPT-5.6 Luna 16,384
  tokens, not just Google's 30k — and the 15s/60s timeouts. The backend-independent prompt cannot
  assume Google capacity. Measure 3–N conversations per supported backend/effective ceiling and set a
  universally safe cap (or provider-specific cap/rejection). This strengthens the case for the
  deferred fan-out (planner → parallel per-conversation fillers, `docs/FANOUT_DESIGN.md`); "one
  conversation per checked title" is a natural fan-out shape, but a quality/latency trade to measure.
- **Conversation count bounds.** Grain guidance ("most contexts have ≥3") sets a floor; the ceiling
  (how many scenes before the phrasebook crowds) needs a real-output number and is bounded by the
  per-backend budget above. Zero checked titles is not a valid generation. With vocab as a top-level
  field, conversations no longer share the eight-group cap.
- **Vocab dedup ownership.** Global pooling across N conversations produces repeats; decide the
  canonical dedup key, whether the service or client dedupes, and the exact `notes` shape for a word
  that appears in several conversations (first source vs. all sources).
- **Suppression-set persistence (expansion-era, not P1).** Retaining unchecked titles as a
  future-expansion suppression set flips the prior "discard all creation state" (J5) decision. Out of
  P1 scope; resolve when conversation expansion is designed. P1 persists the checked titles only.

## Success Criteria

- A generated phrasebook reads as an ordered set of distinct 4–8 turn conversations tracing the
  encounter's before/during/after arc. Cross-conversation redundancy is a measured prompt-quality
  target (semantically interchangeable teaching content), not a hard gate; necessary connective and
  greeting reuse is allowed.
- The Conversations tab is the primary surface; Vocab is a single global list (nouns/adjectives/
  verbs, verbs infinitive, each with a source back-reference); Starred holds both types and is the
  only Review source.
- Growing a phrasebook still requires no filing. (Append/expansion behavior is designed later; P1
  delivers generation + the three-tab review shell.)
- Generation stays within the latency/token budget, or the fan-out is adopted on measured evidence.
