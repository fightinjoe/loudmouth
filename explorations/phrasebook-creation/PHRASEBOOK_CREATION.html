Catchphrase / exploration · iteration 2 · status: exploring

What should creating a phrasebook look like?

Question. The phrasebook view now uses the category-deck design (phrasebook organization, iteration 6). Guided creation still uses the earlier textbook styling, and its form elements are the weakest part. Inside the same action pane, which visual direction should creation take? Iteration 2 combines what worked in iteration 1 and tries three calmer ways to present it.

Boundary. Four phones compare treatments of the same surface and task: describe a situation, answer a few questions, choose conversations, and wait. A is the current web app, unchanged, as the reference. B, C, and D share what iteration 1 kept: one question per page, with Next and Back animating the pages; a white pane with filled rather than outlined fields; the language chosen on the first page from a dropdown; the iteration 1 B conversation list, starting with only the first conversation selected; and a loader that says what is happening. They differ in how bold they are, where the language lives, the answer controls, the page motion, and the loader. Every phone uses one static /context response, whatever you type. Non-goals: prompt or API changes, real requests, cover art, the library, the phrasebook view, and native iOS.

Each phone runs the whole flow on its own: type anything, continue, answer, choose conversations, and create. Mock requests resolve after the selected delay; the log under each phone shows what the real client would request, abort, or reuse. Try changing the language after answering a question in B, C, or D: the situation and answers stay, the questions are refreshed for the new language, and only answers that no longer apply (such as language ability) are asked again. “Show every phone at” puts all four on the same step. Back, Cancel, and Escape close menus, then step back or dismiss; a created phrasebook appears in the stand-in library behind the pane, and “New phrasebook” reopens it. Not implemented: drag-to-dismiss, swipe between pages, real title and cover requests (the title is a fixed mock), saving, and on-screen keyboards.

          Decision record

          Exploring

          StatusExploring (iteration 2). Nothing selected; the current design (A) remains in the web app.
          QuestionWhich visual direction should guided creation take inside the action pane, now that the phrasebook view uses the category-deck design? The main concern is the form elements.
          This iteration’s feedbackAll three iteration 1 directions improved on the current design but fell short. Kept: one context question per screen (A), with Previous and Next animating the content; B’s conversation checklist styling, defaulting to only the first conversation; C’s bolder look, its language selector on the first screen, and its loader, because it leaves room for changing details; D’s language dropdown, though changing it need not reset the flow. Rejected: A’s noise (the see-through background image and outlined form elements); B’s chat framing (an AI styled as a human conversation feels wrong, and content anchored to the bottom made the answer options slow to notice); C’s heavy outlines and large text that hid the prompt, its WYSIWYG title card (the title is rewritten later, the styling felt incongruous, and you edited text below it), and its strongly reinforced chapters, which clash with phrasebooks where topics are a secondary grouping; D’s showing every question at once, its loader inside the button, and conversations popping in.
          HypothesesB tests whether a quiet page with the language woven into the opening sentence keeps the prompt prominent. C tests whether boldness can come from one tinted slab band rather than outlines and type size, and whether the pane-title menu (as in the phrasebook view) is the right home for language. D tests whether a visible trail of answers makes earlier answers quick to revisit and makes changing the language mid-flow feel safe. Loaders: whether listing real milestones (B), rotating through what is being prepared (C), or previewing the category structure (D) makes the wait feel informative. These are hypotheses, not observations.
          Proposals beyond stylingTwo iteration 2 behaviors change the guided creation contract and need a decision before adoption. First conversation only: the client ignores the model’s suggested checked defaults. Language change keeps answers: today a confirmed language change clears context, answers, and the checklist. Here it keeps the situation, requests /context again for the new language, and carries over answers and conversation choices whose labels still match. Ability is per language, so its answer is dropped unless remembered for the new language, and the flow returns to the first unanswered question.

          Implementation handoff

          Data assumptions and behavior

          • Fixture. The /context response is the captured surf-es body in evals/fixtures/captures/baseline-context.json (seed “surf vacation in Costa Rica”, 2 questions, 6 conversations). It is returned for every seed and language. “Long labels” is hand-authored stress content within the context contract limits (5 options, 8 conversations). The mock title “Surfing in Costa Rica” stands in for /phrasebook-title and only appears in the library after saving.
          • Semantics held constant. The ability question (None, Basics, Conversational) is prepended only when ability is unknown and has no Other; context questions start unselected; Next stays disabled until an answer; the speculative /phrasebook request starts when the conversation list appears; changing an answer or the situation aborts it; 1–8 conversations; the final wait lasts at least 1000 ms and overlaps any remaining generation; a failure keeps choices and Retry starts fresh work; dismissal aborts everything. Exceptions in B–D are listed under “Proposals beyond styling”.
          • Loader truth. The API reports no progress inside a request. Milestones in B are real client states: the request was sent, the background result is ready or pending (and since when), and saving follows. C’s rotating panel lists what will be prepared, not progress. D’s category cards are an indeterminate shimmer. Elapsed seconds are real.
          • Page motion. The outgoing page stays in place for about 0.3 s while the incoming page enters: B slides horizontally (back reverses), C rises 40 px and fades (back descends), D crossfades with a slight scale. Only page changes animate; selecting an answer does not. Reduced motion removes all of it. Headers, progress, the trail, and footers stay still.
          • Pane geometry. A matches the current full-height sheet (44 px from the top, 30 px top radius, paper and frog background, header bubble). B–D use the phrasebook pane: 92.68% height, 40 / 62 px radii, white, with the back button and condensed title of the pane header. C’s title menu follows the phrasebook pane’s topic and category menu (aria-haspopup="menu", menuitemradio items, arrow keys, Escape).
          • Retry. After a /context failure, A returns to the situation page as the app does; B–D resubmit the same situation on Try again.
          • Limits. The /context seed limit is 200 characters before trimming; B–D enforce it with maxlength and B shows the count. The current app does not limit the field.
