Catchphrase / interaction study / exploring

A phrase you can
see inside.

Can a learner understand how a useful phrase is composed without leaving the conversation or opening a miniature textbook?

The clicked card grows into a modal: select a phrase part, inspect its explanation, or SHOW ALL. This is the retained exploration direction, not an approved production pane pattern.

Retained direction / expanding card modal

The card becomes the explanation.

Click a card to grow it into a modal. Close it to shrink back into its place in the conversation.

Click anywhere on a conversation card or focus it and press Enter / Space. SHOW ALL reveals all explanation cards; SHOW SELECTED restores your selection. Selecting a sentence part returns to one explanation. Close, Escape, or the scrim dismiss the modal. Motion respects reduced-motion preferences. Study controls reset to the closed conversation. Audio, stars, saving, character drill-down, generated conversations, and native-platform integration are not implemented. English explanations remain visible when phrase translations are hidden.

Decision record

Teach the relationship, not just the words.

Status & boundary
Exploring; expanding-card direction retained by user preference. The action-pane specimen has been removed, not hidden. Clicking a conversation card grows it into a modal. This still changes the current card-tap pronunciation contract and needs a separate production layer decision. Audio, saving, stars, character inspection, and conversation generation are discussion proposals only.
Working preference
User feedback retains the expanding-card modal with inline selection. SHOW ALL now integrates that lesson’s useful part: every explanation card appears in sentence order. SHOW SELECTED restores the last individual selection; clicking a sentence part also switches to its one explanation. Each part now has 4 px horizontal padding and a reserved 2 px transparent bottom border; selection colors that border, text, and background without shifting layout. All parts highlight in all-mode. The resting cards use the same padded segmentation so opening does not suddenly spread the phrase apart. Every explanation card now contains its English meaning, the exact selected fragment with available readings, grammatical role, and explanation. The card shares the selection’s blue background, including during hover. Part count and SHOW ALL / SHOW SELECTED follow the explanation list rather than interrupting the phrase-to-card relationship. A one-part phrase omits the redundant toggle.
Comparison tradeoff
Archived directions: the linear lesson duplicated content, so its exhaustive view became SHOW ALL. The separate action-pane specimen was subsequently removed at user request. Keep the expanding card as the only live study. Its strengths and risks remain hypotheses: continuity with the source card, a narrower reading surface, and a conflict with pronunciation on the outer conversation card. Previous treatments remain in version history.
State & geometry
The modal floats with 12 px side margins, 36 px top/bottom margins, and 20 px corners. It has no title, header divider, or “The phrase” label; its phrase starts at the same 16 px inset as the resting card. Close stays in the bottom-right corner, with bottom scroll padding to keep content reachable. The same card node grows over 400 ms, interpolating position, dimensions, speaker-colored background to white, and 16→20 px corners without scaling text. Explanation content fades in after 140 ms with a 6 px settling movement, while the phrase remains visible throughout. A same-sized slot preserves the conversation layout; closing reverses to it. Reduced motion skips the animations. Reopen resets selection/all-mode. Part hit areas include 4 px horizontal padding but do not guarantee a 44 px minimum; touch usability remains open.
Modal behavior
The conversation is inert while the modal is open. Focus enters Close; Tab cycles inside; Escape, Close, and the scrim dismiss; focus returns to the original card without scrolling it. Conversation cards also open with Enter/Space. Only one expansion is allowed; closing during growth cancels growth before returning. There is no action pane or drag-to-dismiss handle in this study. Production must resolve layer ownership and exclusivity before adoption.
Future review cards
Proposal, not implemented: the repeated fragment makes each explanation identifiable away from the full sentence. Preserve its language, contextual meaning, reading, grammatical role, explanation, and source sentence if saving is later adopted. Do not treat every chunk as an independent dictionary word: a particle, conjugated ending, or permission construction may need a sentence-based review prompt instead of an isolated translation. Keep the exact surface form linked to its source; decide separately whether to teach a dictionary form or a reusable construction. No Save control, persistence, new schema, or review behavior is implied by this visual study.
Discussion proposals
Not implemented: tap an explanation’s language area to hear the displayed form; Save keeps an item, Star includes it in review (starring an unsaved item also saves it). Show the reusable word and encountered inflection explicitly rather than silently replacing one with the other. A Characters disclosure within the selected chunk could reveal contextual kanji/hanzi meanings without changing sentence segmentation; kana inflection is not character vocabulary. Consider Parts / Pattern views beneath the source so grammar is a peer rather than a distant footer. Word/phrase, character, and pattern cards need different review prompts but can share a visual shell. Keep another example separate from a future “Practice this pattern in a conversation” action: generate a preview, then explicitly add a group. This would extend the current bounded phrasebook-generation model.
Data assumptions
Hand-authored fixture analysis is separate from persisted cards. Cards retain text, translation, optional reading / romanization, and JSON-string notes with actual speaker and or: true metadata. Reading tokens are pronunciation units, not grammar segmentation. This study uses ordered semantic chunks plus contextual meaning, role, explanation, and a phrase-level pattern. A production contract must decide span alignment, overlapping constructions, provenance, and stale-analysis invalidation; do not infer these from ruby boundaries or add schema fields by copying this fixture.
Unavailable content
“Not available” preserves the source phrase and clearly states that this fixture has no analysis. It offers no fake retry. A real loading/error/retry contract requires a separately designed analysis endpoint. This study makes no API or generation-speed claim.
Language order
English precedes the target-language phrase in conversation cards and the modal. Font styling is unchanged. Ruby is above the characters: pinyin for Chinese and furigana for Japanese. The separate Japanese romaji line is omitted; fixture romanization remains data, not a second visible representation. Working recommendation: Japanese script with optional furigana by default, with a mutually exclusive romaji mode considered separately for learners who cannot read kana. No romaji-mode control is implemented in this iteration. The earlier under-ruby experiment was reverted at user request. Visually verified Japanese furigana above the characters in Chromium, with zero romaji rows and English-first order; no console errors.
Observed
Consolidation verified in gstack headless Chromium on macOS: one live specimen, no action pane or handle. Opened the modal, selected a fragment, switched SHOW ALL/SELECTED, expanded the renamed example disclosure, and dismissed with Escape; focus returned to the original card. Visually inspected Japanese at 320 px / 150%, with furigana, no romaji, and no horizontal body overflow. No console errors. Earlier iterations verified matching selection/card colors, contextual fragment readings, animation continuity, and conversation scroll restoration.
Open checks
Physical iPhone Safari, native Dynamic Type, VoiceOver/TalkBack, real touch interaction, OS reduced motion, resize during animation, and real-model analysis remain unverified. Need learner feedback on character depth, useful review units, grammar discoverability, and whether the growing card feels continuous. Audio, star/save semantics, and generated practice conversations have not been implemented or tested. No learning-effectiveness claim.
Handoff
Follow methodology, product scope, UX design, card schema, and Pane Protocol. The retained modal needs an explicit container/layer decision and resolution of card-tap pronunciation before production adoption; do not add an ad-hoc fifth layer. Native adoption needs independent verification. Current persisted card types are word, phrase, and sentence; character/pattern types and per-item saving require a separate contract decision.
