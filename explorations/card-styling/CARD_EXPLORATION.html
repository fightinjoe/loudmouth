Three ways to gather.

Conversation topics, vocabulary, and starred cards serve different reading rhythms. Explore each in context, with shared language and typography controls—not three competing skins for the same list.

Exploration, not a production specification. Standalone, offline, no build step. Open this file directly in a browser.

Star any conversation or vocabulary card to add it to C; unstar it to remove it. Two cards start starred in each content set. State is local to this page and resets on reload. No audio or card-opening interaction is simulated. On small screens, previews shrink to fit; on desktop, an oversized preview scrolls inside its column.

Evaluation

Three groupings, three reading rhythms.

Questions to answer

  • A: does side alone make turn-taking clear, without assigning either role to the learner?
  • Does “or” connect two choices for the same speaker rather than look like another turn?
  • B: does the loud, borderless vocabulary collage make words memorable, or distract from reading?
  • C: can phrases and vocabulary sit together naturally without implying a conversation?
  • Do pinyin and furigana remain attached to their characters when wrapping?

Stress pass

  1. View each language at 390 px, then long content at 320 px and 150% text.
  2. Swap conversation sides. Both options must move together; neither gets a role label.
  3. Hide readings and English. Check spacing with and without ruby.
  4. Star items in A and B, then remove every item from C. Check the empty state.
  5. Use Tab and Space to toggle stars. Reset restores the initial selection.
Decisions from the first pass: keep offset bubbles for conversation; remove role labels. Reject the bordered full-width treatment—it interrupts flow. Reserve the ruled list for starred cards, not dialogue. The new vocabulary collage is deliberately extreme and remains an experiment.
Reusable format

One question. A runnable reference.

Follow the Pane Protocol model: a live example next to the reasoning. Unlike that settled protocol, this study presents alternatives; it does not establish application rules.

  1. Frame: name the surface, its grouping semantics, and non-goals.
  2. Explore: keep language and typography controls shared; use identical fixtures when comparing alternatives for the same purpose. Do not confuse conversation, vocabulary, and starred collections.
  3. Stress: include long and short content, alternatives, empty collections, narrow widths, and larger type.
  4. Observe: record device/browser, settings, and the specific failure or advantage.
  5. Decide: record accepted, rejected, and unresolved ideas before porting. Keep the runnable specimens as a visual reference.

Current scope: conversational topic groups, vocabulary, and starred cards in Spanish, simplified Chinese with ruby pinyin, and Japanese with furigana or romaji. Out of scope: details panes, swipe actions, audio, backend/schema changes, and production integration.

Future studies: follow the shared exploration methodology. Create a separate directory under explorations/ for each concept, keeping its standalone HTML, fixtures, and assets local so studies can evolve independently.

Porting reference

Preserve hierarchy, not CSS.

  • Anatomy: target with optional ruby above → optional romaji below → English. No speaker heading; Spanish has no empty reading line. Chinese pinyin and Japanese furigana use the same ruby layout.
  • Typography baseline: Latin target 20 px; CJK target 23 px; romaji 13 px; English 14 px; ruby 0.48 em. The size control scales all text. These are exploration values, not approved native tokens.
  • Conversation: side and tint distinguish two interchangeable voices. The swap control exchanges sides, not content or order. Star emphasis changes the icon, never speaker identity.
  • Alternatives: the fixture uses API-shaped JSON-encoded notes, such as {"speaker":"you","or":true}. A literal “or” precedes that option on the same side as the preceding option. This study exercises adjacent alternatives; the API also allows interleaved alternatives, which need a separate grouping decision before production adoption. See card schema.
  • Starred collection: C combines selected phrases and words from the active fixture, retaining a small source-context caption but no speaker or “or” marker. Removing a star removes that entry; empty selection is an intentional state.
  • Interaction: stars have 44 × 44 CSS-pixel targets and pressed state. Production plays audio on card-body taps; this artifact limits interaction to controls and stars.
  • Reading data: hand-authored base/annotation pairs, not automatic syllable alignment. Kana and punctuation without annotations remain ordinary text. Chinese syllables are individually paired with characters so long phrases can wrap without detaching readings.
  • Native checks still required: actual iOS Safari ruby wrapping, VoiceOver reading order, Dynamic Type, and native font metrics. Browser scaling is not Dynamic Type. Have a fluent reviewer check fixtures before using them as teaching content.

Production references: web/app/src/components/card.js and conversation styles in web/app/src/styles/components.css. A borrows the current 88% sided bubbles but removes role labels. Production files are unchanged.
