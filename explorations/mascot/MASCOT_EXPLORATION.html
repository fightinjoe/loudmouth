Catchphrase / mascot study · iteration 4 · exploring

Who shows up
when you open the app?

Question. Kero is the preferred character and Bub the runner-up, and their appeal is their flat simplicity. Can each become a true isometric object, a flat drawing turned 30° and given thickness like a Mac OS 8 “Copland” icon, without losing that simplicity? A frog-eyed Bub (Bub with flat Kero’s eye bumps) is the fourth character. The character must work on the home screen, in the browser tab, beside the wordmark on the navigation pane, and while a phrasebook is being written, without suggesting scores, streaks, or a curriculum (brief: no pressure, no gamification).

Boundary. Five options in the same four contexts: Kero and frog-eyed Bub, each flat and isometric, plus flat Bub. All art is hand-authored flat 32×32 grids. Each isometric version is computed in the page from its flat grid by a fixed projection (2:1 shear, re-outline, extrusion), so editing a flat grid updates both. Every flat option has a 16×16 favicon; isometric options reuse their flat source’s. Not in scope: scenery, a bug-net prop or tongue catch (paused until the isometric base is right), a final wordmark, colour-system changes, and native iOS asset catalogs. The contexts are static mock-ups; nothing navigates or saves.

Home-screen wallpaper Projection Faces Thickness

Animation starts off when the system asks for reduced motion. Projection, facing, and thickness apply to every isometric view on the page. “Isometric” lays the face on a 30° (2:1) plane, so the face slants; “Oblique” keeps the face exactly the flat drawing and adds only the 30° depth. “Faces” sets which way the front looks (the side face appears opposite); “Thickness” is the extrusion in steps of 2 px across and 1 px up. Hover a large sprite to read a pixel’s coordinate and palette key, for feedback like “move the pupil at 21,9”. Frame thumbnails are clickable while animation is off.

Where the character has to work

Icons are shown at 60 px as on an iPhone home screen: flat art on a 36×36 tile (2 art pixels of padding); isometric art fills its 48×48 tile. The navigation header uses the converged layout from the navigation-pane study with flat characters at 2× (64 px) and isometric ones at 1.5× (72 px, whole device pixels only on 2× screens). The loading state is the creation sheet’s minimum 1000 ms wait (PHRASEBOOK_MIN_LOADING_MS) with every character at 96 px.

Browser tab · 16×16 favicon

Stress cases

Smallest sizes, masks, and single-colour use

“Maskable” crops the full-bleed tile to the 80 % circle every Android launcher mask must preserve (the dashed square is the uncropped tile); the current manifest declares purpose: "any maskable" for one icon. “Silhouette” is the shape alone, as in a pinned-tab or notification glyph. “Tinted” approximates iOS 18 tinted icons by converting to luminance on a dark ground; it is not Apple’s actual algorithm.

Decision record

Status: exploring · iteration 4

StatusExploring. Kero preferred; Bub runner-up; Folio archived. Isometric versions rebuilt as projections of the flat drawings; flat frog-eyed Bub added as their source.
QuestionCan flat Kero and a frog-eyed Bub become true 30° isometric objects, in the manner of Mac OS 8 Copland icons, while keeping the simplicity of the flat drawings?
FeedbackIteration 1: the user preferred Kero, then Bub, and asked to remove Folio. Their original idea was a bug net, to convey catching phrases; Kero fits because frogs catch bugs the same way. They asked to see Kero isometrically.
Iteration 2: missed the mark. The lily pad was unnecessary, the tongue did not reach far enough, and the version was too cute and too detailed; the user prefers flat Kero’s simplicity and few colours. They asked for a fourth option: Bub’s styling in isometric view with frog eyes on top. Mid-drafting, white eyeballs on blue domes looked too much like Cookie Monster.
Iteration 3: still missed. Neither version was really isometric: the user means a much more skewed, 30° view reminiscent of Mac OS 8 Copland file-system icons. Flat Kero’s eyes are bumps poking out of the top of his outline; isometric Bub had balls for eyes, which looked creepy and lost his flat, cute eyes, and his mouth at an odd angle made him look like a cheap muppet. Isometric Kero looked like a baby; he should be as robust as flat Kero.
Options
  • Kero · isometric. Flat Kero projected onto a 2:1 plane and extruded (default 3 steps). The top face takes his belly green and the side his shadow green, so the palette stays flat Kero’s eight colours. His proportions, domes, and frames are the flat ones.
  • Bub × Kero · isometric. The flat frog-eyed Bub projected the same way. The top face is light blue and the side dark blue: Bub’s palette only.
  • Bub × Kero · flat (new). Bub’s body, palette, flat eyes, and smile, with two eye bumps in the outline like flat Kero’s domes. Hand-drawn 32×32 with smile, talk, and blink frames and a 16×16 favicon.
  • Kero · flat and Bub from iteration 1, unchanged.
ObservationIteration 4, verified 2026-09-28 in gstack’s headless Chromium at 1320 px and 390 px, 2× device scale, zero console errors. Every frame of all five sequences was observed within 5 s. The projection, facing, and thickness controls repaint heroes, thumbnails, icons, headers, loading sheets, and stress cells; Reset returns to isometric, right, and 3. No horizontal page overflow at 390 px.
  • Isometric. Both characters now sit on a true 2:1 plane with a lit top face and a dark side face. Kero keeps flat Kero’s proportions and domes, and Bub’s eyes stay flat. The cost is inherent: both faces slant with the plane, so one eye sits lower and the smile tilts. Facing right reads better than facing left because Bub’s tail stays a tail; facing left flattens it into a spur.
  • Oblique. The faces are exactly the flat drawings, with the 30° thickness behind them. The characters are the most recognisably the flat Kero and Bub of any 3D treatment so far. Facing left suits it: the side face falls to the right, away from Bub’s tail.
  • Small sizes. At 60 px both 3D treatments still read, but the slab makes the characters smaller in the tile than the flat ones. The isometric silhouettes lose their characters (a lump and a leaning bubble), so flat versions remain necessary for favicons and glyphs.
  • Frog-eyed Bub, flat. Reads as a character at every size down to its 16×16. It fills its icon tile more than flat Bub and has a clearer silhouette.
Iteration 1 (same browser and widths): exercised Animate, thumbnail pick, pixel grid, dark wallpaper, hover readout, and Reset.
  • 16×16. Bub reads immediately as a bubble with a face. Kero reads as a frog but its eyes become two white bars.
  • Silhouette. Bub is the only one recognisable as a solid shape; Kero is an eared blob.
  • Maskable 80 % circle. Both clip: Bub loses its tail and corners, Kero its feet. A padded maskable variant is required; the 2-pixel tile padding only suits iOS-style rounded squares.
  • Home screen. Both hold up on light and dark wallpapers; Bub on white has stronger tile contrast than Kero on blue-100.
  • Navigation header. At 64 px beside the 24 px wordmark, Bub doubles the blue and competes with the wordmark; Kero contrasts with it.
Not verified: reduced-motion default (read from prefers-reduced-motion in markup only), keyboard use of the frame thumbnails, VoiceOver, and any physical device.
DecisionFolio archived (iteration 1). Iteration 2’s lily-pad Kero rejected (iteration 2 feedback). Iteration 3’s ray-marched isometric Kero and ball-eyed Bub rejected (iteration 3 feedback). Current candidates: none selected, awaiting feedback.
Rejected while drafting
  • A side-view frog catching a bubble with its tongue: the tongue needed half the canvas, leaving the frog too small to read at 32 px. Replaced with the front-facing croak, where the sac is the bubble.
  • Folio, the walking phrasebook (iteration 1): a coral pocket book with legs, a speech-bubble cover badge, and a blue ribbon. Archived because the user preferred Kero and Bub. Its grids were removed before this study was first committed.
  • Iteration 2’s isometric Kero: lily pad, ripple, winged phrase, three shades of green plus belly shading. Too cute, too detailed, and its tongue was about 8 px. Its grids were replaced in this file.
  • Iteration 3’s isometric Kero and Bub, ray-marched from 3D shapes and turned only 34° towards the viewer: not skewed enough to read as isometric. Bub’s spherical eyes read as creepy, his slanted mouth as a muppet’s, and Kero’s rounder body as a baby. Replaced by projecting the flat drawings, which keeps their eyes, mouths, and proportions.
  • Projection with half-pixel depth steps: the top and side bands striped, and the bottom edge grew a comb of teeth. Depth now steps a whole 2 px across and 1 px up.
  • Shearing the flat outline as-is: diagonal edges and Bub’s thin tail broke into alternating pixels. The outline is now dropped, the sheared shape’s 1-px notches closed, and a clean outline redrawn.
