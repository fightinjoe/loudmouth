Catchphrase / exploration · iteration 6 · status: category decks selected and adopted in web app

Topic decks, or category decks?

Question. Iteration 6: after using the web app, organizing by category reads as more valuable than organizing by topic. If the landing page leads with the four categories (Words, Key phrases, Exchanges, Conversation) and each category gathers every topic’s content, is it easier to study and find things than the standardized topic decks? Iteration 5 asked whether the topic card reads at a glance, whether the header should switch from bubble to bar at one scroll point, and whether a menu in the pane header makes moving between topics easy.

Boundary. Two phones with the same content, stars, and page-wide controls (including accent). The first is the standardized topic organization already in the web app. The second, new in iteration 6, organizes by category: Words and Exchanges cards are headed by their group (word family or exchange title), and Key phrases and Conversation cards by their topic. Both share the collapsing title, Starred card, spaced Phrases cards, and vertical exchanges. Alternatives from earlier iterations are retired; their rationale is kept in the decision record. Non-goals: audio, phrase breakdown, Review, reordering, CJK readings, library navigation, and native iOS.

Accent theming

One accent, derived tokens

Each phrasebook supplies one accent color. Every themed surface is derived from it with color-mix(in oklab, accent N%, white), so a new phrasebook needs only that value:

TokenDerivationUsed for
--accentThe phrasebook’s accentSection labels, title text, active tab, filled stars, numbers, back icon
--accent-280% accentSecondary actions: “In context”, “Save exchange”
--accent-soft24% accentLanguage pill, bubble bottom, spaced-card edge
--accent-wash8% accentBubble top, strip header band, “Open” pill
--starbg / --starline11% / 22% accentStarred card background and border

The control reports the accent’s contrast on white; labels are 14 px, so they need at least 4.5:1. Stars, icons, and the bubble are CSS masks or inline SVG so they follow the accent. The illustration stays as authored. “Tint art to accent” rotates its hue toward the accent to preview a matching art set; it is a simulation, not a production technique.

Implementation handoff

Data assumptions and geometry

  • Data. Fixture follows phrasebook v4: ordered topics, each with Things to say (shown as “Phrases”), Likely exchanges, Word families, and an Example conversation. Exact-English cross-topic Things to say duplicates are removed, as at commit. The English is the surf-beginner-es evaluation; the Spanish is hand-authored and illustrative.
  • Stars. A star belongs to the durable Phrase or Word (keyed by exact English here) and updates every occurrence. Whole-exchange saving uses the exchange’s durable ID as a new kind of membership; this is a schema proposal.
  • Layers. The illustration (Figma image 46/47, at −139, 78, masked by the ragged Rectangle 2) and the language pill scroll with the content; the white reading surface starts at 392 px. The bubble title is a fixed layer in front. It switches to the collapsed bar when the content scroll passes 150 px and back below 110 px; the gap prevents flicker when the scroll position rests near the threshold.
  • Category cards. 196 × 168 px (scaled with text size, capped at 62% of the phone width), no stack layers. Cards in one category share a group with a 16 px radius, a 1 px gray-200 border, and 1 px dividers; groups are 8 px apart and each card is a snap point. A Words card opens Words and scrolls to its family; an Exchanges card opens Exchanges at its exchange; topic headers open the remembered category.
  • Pane topic menu. A button with aria-haspopup="menu" and aria-expanded opens a menu of menuitemradio items (current one checked). Arrow keys, Home, and End move focus; Escape or a tap outside closes it and returns focus to the title. Choosing an item replaces the pane content in place without closing the pane; Back still returns focus to the original topic.
  • Remembered category. The pane opens on the category last shown in any topic of that phrasebook, for this session. Choosing a deck updates it. Persisting it across sessions is a product decision left open.
  • Exchanges. Each turn is one bubble aligned to its speaker (learner left on #f9fafb; partner right, white with a gray-200 border, 86% width), with alternatives stacked inside and a labeled “or” divider. Each alternative keeps its own star.
  • Pinned headings. Word family titles and exchange titles are position: sticky inside their own group, so the next heading pushes the previous one out; a 10 px white edge covers the page’s top padding.
